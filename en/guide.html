Using VideoGlance

VideoGlance adds Quick Look previews and Finder thumbnails for videos that macOS does not preview directly. The application is planned to be free; optional support will not unlock extra features.

This guide describes the controls confirmed by the owner on build 32 and the Finder checks on 1.1, build 42, now installed on the reference Mac. The local build 43 adds third-party credits without changing playback. A signed disk image has been submitted to Apple; no public download or working Homebrew command is available yet.

Current compatibility

The package declares a technical minimum of macOS 15.0. The local version has been tried on macOS 15.7.9, on an Apple Silicon Mac. Other macOS 15 versions and Intel Macs have not been validated; macOS 14 is not covered by the current dependencies. The declared formats are MKV, WebM, AVI, FLV/F4V, WMV, OGV/OGM, TS/MTS/M2TS and RM/RMVB. Tests include MKV H.264 and WebM VP9, with an extended media corpus with 23 cases on the current playback engine. A file's container does not guarantee that every track and codec it contains can be played.

MP4, MOV, M4V and 3GP previews remain provided by macOS. Double-clicking a video continues to open its default application.

Installation

Public distribution is in preparation. The download and Homebrew cask will be documented here after verification; no public installation command is available yet. Once the application is installed, macOS requests previews and thumbnails as needed; you do not need to keep the VideoGlance information window open.

The local build includes a Third-party notices button in that window. It opens the texts for the libraries included in the application. These notices are available offline. A separate Terms of use button opens the approved French and English terms offline.

Open a video in Quick Look

  1. Select a video in Finder.
  2. Press Space, or use Command-Y.
  3. Playback starts after the window opens.
  4. Press Space or Escape to close the preview.

Finder manages navigation between files and full screen. The arrow keys are not reassigned to video seeking. To open the preview directly in full screen, use Option-Command-Y.

Play a video in Finder's Preview pane

The Preview pane is the area on the right of the Finder window, separate from column view. Show it with View → Show Preview or Shift-Command-P, then select a video. It was checked in list and icon views on the reference Mac; column view also keeps the embedded control previously confirmed on build 32.

The video starts as a still image. Move the pointer over the image to reveal the central button, then click its center to play or pause.

During playback, the ring shows progress. Click or drag within this ring to change position without pausing the video. The center of the button retains its Play/Pause action. This view does not show the full control bar, volume or speed menu.

Quick Look window controls

The bar appears when the pointer moves over the video. It stays hidden on opening if the pointer is elsewhere, and hides quickly when the pointer leaves the image, during playback or while paused. During playback, it can also hide after a period of inactivity within the image. Keeping the pointer over the controls or using them keeps the bar visible.

At the end, the video stops. Click Play to start again from the beginning. Finder's closing shortcuts remain available after using the buttons, volume or progress bar.

Finder thumbnails

The extension provides a representative image to Finder views that use thumbnails, preserving the video's aspect ratio. Finder adds the frame and icon effects. Files are read directly; no full-video copy is prepared to display a preview.

The thumbnail helps identify a file among others. The Preview pane is a separate presentation of the selected file: contents, playback controls and information. It does more than enlarge the thumbnail. The chosen image can differ: for one 20-second MP4 reference, the icon showed 00:10 and the initial pane showed 00:00; the corresponding MKV had the same distinction. This observation on macOS 15.7.9 is not a universal frame-selection rule. Finder's information and Quick Actions depend on the file type; VideoGlance does not add Apple's trimming tools to MKVs.

A thumbnail already on screen may remain cached after an update. This does not necessarily mean that the new extension has not loaded. When reporting a problem, note whether it also affects a newly added file.

Known limitations

The appearance aims to be close to MP4 previews, but some colors and transparency effects still differ. When navigating from MKV to MP4 and back to the same MKV, a cached preview may resume at its previous position. This limitation is accepted for the first public release. An automatic restart is therefore not guaranteed; move the playhead to 00:00 in the Quick Look window to restart manually.

This player does not provide trimming, AirPlay, Picture in Picture, DRM, network streams or manual selection of audio/subtitle tracks. Native macOS controls remain available for formats that macOS supports itself.

Troubleshooting

Reports can be opened in the public site issue tracker. Avoid sharing personal videos or sensitive information.

  • No preview after an update: close the preview and try again. If the issue persists, report the installed version and exact message. The development installer registers both extensions when replacing the application.
  • A video will not open: provide its container, codecs if known, duration, and whether other files in the same format work.
  • Appearance or delay differs from an MP4: specify the Finder view, action, Play/Pause state, display and animation settings used.
  • An old thumbnail remains: distinguish a cached image from a thumbnail generated for a new copy. Avoid a global cache purge as the first diagnostic.

For a report, provide your macOS version, Mac type, VideoGlance version/build and the steps to reproduce the issue. A small synthetic file that reproduces the problem is preferable to a personal video.

Uninstalling and support

The Homebrew procedure and the future package's uninstall instructions will be added after those paths have been validated. For the current local installation, close previews and move VideoGlance.app from Applications to the Trash. This removes the container for its two extensions; Finder may temporarily retain a view or thumbnail that it has already loaded. This procedure still needs to be included in release testing on a clean Mac.

The first release has no donation link. Optional support may be added later. No payment account is needed to use VideoGlance.
